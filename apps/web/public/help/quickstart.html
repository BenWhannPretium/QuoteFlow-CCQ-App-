QuoteFlow quick-start: KAM

What QuoteFlow is for you. One place to request a commercial capital quote and follow it to the customer's answer. You start from your Salesforce opportunity. Engineering, Pricing and Final Review do their part in QuoteFlow, and you see where it is at every step.

Your home screen: My quotes. It shows what is Returned to me, My drafts, My open quotes by stage, Proposals expiring soon and My outcome capture rate. Your menu: Home, New intake, My proposals, Pipeline.

What you'll do

  1. Start an intake. Click New intake, find your Salesforce opportunity and click Use this opportunity. Four steps: Opportunity, Customer & ship-to, Product & volume, Drawing, notes & review. Click Submit to CCQ. You get a quote number like CCQ-2026-00042.
  2. Attach the drawing, or give the minimum spec. On step 4, drag files onto Drawing and other files, or tap Add photo on your phone. Without a drawing you must give the minimum spec on step 3 (height, width or diameter, depth, gram weight, neck, resin, color), or the quote cannot be submitted.
  3. Tell Pricing what it needs. On step 3, under Commercial inputs for Pricing, say what payment terms the customer asks for, whether the customer pays for any of the tooling (Not discussed is the default), whether it wants returnable (multi-trip) dunnage, and whether the price is Delivered or FOB Pretium plant (with the freight type and any special arrangement, such as a drop trailer or T-bars). Add a Bid / program reference if there is one. All optional: a blank shows as a warning you can ignore. A payment term other than Net 15 or Net 30 shows Non-standard: Pricing will review.
  4. Fix a returned quote. If a sales leader or Engineering returns it, you get a notification. Open the quote and click Fix and resubmit. The quote keeps its number. If Pricing sent it back, you are asked whether the scope changed: No, same scope sends it straight back to Pricing; Yes sends it through qualification and Engineering again.
  5. Record the customer's answer. Go to My proposals and click Record outcome: Awarded, In negotiation, Lost, No response / Expired, On hold or Re-quote. No response is not a loss.
  6. Hand off a win. After Awarded, fill the Commit & hand off card (CER and receiving team), then click Commit & hand off → Advance (close Won).

What changed from the ERF

  • Every quote starts from a Salesforce opportunity. One quote per opportunity.
  • You no longer pick the plant, the engineer or the priority. Engineering decides the plant and engineer. The priority score is calculated and advisory.
  • The customer is the Salesforce Account on the opportunity. You don't create customer accounts: tick Not yet a Pretium customer (no ERP account) if that applies.
  • You give Quote needed by and Product needed by dates. The Salesforce close date is shown but not typed.
  • You can't change the priority tier. Ask your sales leader to override it.
  • Awarded is the customer's yes. The quote closes Won only after Commit & Hand Off.
  • You can see the price approval, pricing basis and capital figures on your quotes. Only Pricing changes them.
  • Once a gate is decided, its section is locked until a send-back reopens it.
  • After you record an outcome, QuoteFlow reminds you to update the Salesforce opportunity. It does not write to Salesforce.

  • Tre Curry, coordination: access, sessions, follow-ups.
  • Ben Whann, builder: anything broken.
  • Found a problem? Click Report an issue in your user menu or at the bottom of any page.

QuoteFlow quick-start: Sales leader

What QuoteFlow is for you. You decide which new opportunities are worth engineering time (gate G1, Qualify & prioritize). You approve priority changes and see your team's pipeline and outcomes.

Your home screen: Sales pipeline. It shows To qualify (G1), Pending override requests (only requests raised before the change), Team pipeline by stage and Proposals with no outcome past validity. Your menu: Home, New intake, My proposals, To qualify, Pipeline, Approvals, Analytics.

What you'll do

  1. Qualify new intakes. Click To qualify. The list is highest priority first. Click Decide and choose Advance, Hold, Rework (back to KAM), Redirect (not CCQ work) or Stop. Click Record decision. The ⓘ next to each choice explains it.
  2. Override a priority tier. You are the only role that can. On the quote click Override priority tier, pick the tier and give a reason of 15 or more characters. It applies at once and Engineering sees your reason on its queue. To undo it, open the quote's Timeline and click Revert.
  3. Watch your team's pipeline. Use Pipeline (List, Prioritization, Board) and Analytics. Each chart has an ⓘ with its definition.
  4. Chase missing outcomes. Proposals with no outcome past validity on your home screen. You can record an outcome for your team.

What changed from the ERF

  • Qualification is a recorded decision with a reason, not an email.
  • The priority score orders the list. You decide.
  • You cannot qualify a deal where you are the KAM. Another sales leader decides it.
  • KAMs, Engineering and Pricing can't change the priority tier. They ask you.
  • You can read pricing and capital figures on any quote you can open. You cannot change them.

QuoteFlow quick-start: Engineering lead

What QuoteFlow is for you. One queue for all upfront engineering work on new business quotes. You assign it, sign off capacity and decide whether there is a feasible solution (gate G2, Feasible solution?).

Your home screen: Engineering queue. It shows Queue health, Open load per engineer, Time to assign and to G2 and Returned to the KAM: top reasons. You are alerted (bell and email) when a quote arrives. Your menu: Home, Engineering queue, Pipeline, Approvals, Analytics.

What you'll do

  1. Assign work. Engineering queue → row menu … → Assign… (or tick rows and Assign selected…). Use Reassign work… to move someone's open work.
  2. Return incomplete intakes. Row menu → Return to KAM…. Say what is missing.
  3. Sign off capacity. On the quote's Engineering tab click Approve capacity, or Block capacity with a note. G2 Advance needs capacity approved.
  4. Decide G2. Engineering tab → Decide G2…: Advance, Advance with risk, Advance – capital required, Hold, Rework (back to KAM), Redirect (alternative platform / site) or Stop.
  5. Keep order. Pin to top… puts a quote first, with a reason. It is logged. You cannot change the priority tier: only a sales leader can.
  6. Review what your engineers submit. Ready for my review lists quotes they submitted to you. Decide G2, or Send back to engineer… with a note.

What changed from the ERF

  • The queue is sorted by tier, score and age. You still assign by hand.
  • You pick the engineer and the plant. The KAM no longer does.
  • Capacity evidence and your sign-off are part of the G2 record. Utilization after the new business is calculated, not typed.
  • Once you decide G2, the engineering section is locked until a send-back reopens it.
  • A hold, a capital-required decision, advance-with-risk and every send-back need a note.
  • Returns go back to the KAM with a reason, and the quote keeps its number.

QuoteFlow quick-start: Engineer

What QuoteFlow is for you. The engineering reviews assigned to you, with the intake, the drawing and the KAM's notes in one place. You record the solution and the capacity evidence. Your lead decides G2.

Your home screen: My assignments. Your reviews are listed first, then the whole queue (read-only). You get a notification and an email when work is assigned to you. Your menu: Home, My assignments, Pipeline.

What you'll do

  1. Accept the review. Open the quote → Engineering tab → Accept (start review).
  2. Record the solution. Fill Process type, Machine type, Plant / site, Cavitation, Cycle time (s), Budgetary capital estimate (USD) and the notes. Click Save solution. The product facts from intake are shown at the top: you don't retype them. Upload the equipment BOM (the one-page CSR BOM) in its own card; a new upload keeps the earlier versions. It is needed before your lead can advance G2 as “Capital required”. If the part is made in two steps, tick Two-step process and fill the plants and preform number; add the pack-out counts (units per bag, tray, case, pallet) if you have them. Pricing reads both.
  3. Add capacity evidence. Add capacity row: site, line, utilization before, allocated annual volume, run days, assumed effectiveness, cycle time, analysis date, analyst. Click Save capacity evidence. Utilization after is calculated for you. Download CSV exports the rows; Attach analysis adds your own file.
  4. Attach files. Attachments tab: drag CAD files, PDFs or photos onto the drop area.
  5. Submit to your lead. When your part is ready, click Submit to engineering lead. It is locked for you until they decide or send it back.

What changed from the ERF

  • Work comes to you by assignment, with an alert, not by email.
  • Capacity evidence is recorded with the quote, not in a separate file.
  • You can read back every figure you enter, and the pricing on the quote. Only Pricing changes pricing.

Who to ask

  • Your engineering lead: what to work on next.
  • Bailey Schmitt, product owner: rules and definitions.
  • Tre Curry, coordination: access. Ben Whann, builder: anything broken.

QuoteFlow quick-start: Pricing and the pricing lead

What QuoteFlow is for you. The quotes Engineering has cleared, ready to price. You upload the costing model, record the price approval and capital readiness, and the pricing lead approves the price (gate G3, Price approved) to send the quote to Final Review. Costing stays in Excel and the pricing app; QuoteFlow keeps the files. Capital readiness is its own field, not part of the gate.

Your home screen. Analysts see Pricing desk. The pricing lead sees Pricing triage. Both show Quoting queue, Capital readiness pending and Median time in Quoting. Your menu: Home, Pricing queue, Pipeline, Analytics.

What you'll do

  1. Pricing lead: assign pricers. Pricing queue → row menu … → Assign pricer…. Use Reassign work… to balance the load.
  2. Read what the KAM told you. On the Quoting tab, From intake shows the payment terms the customer asked for (a non-standard request is flagged Non-standard: Pricing will review: it is for awareness and negotiation and does not change the costing), whether the customer funds any tooling, returnable or single-trip dunnage, delivered or FOB with the freight type and any special arrangement (drop trailers and T-bars are costly), and the bid / program reference. A blank means the KAM did not say: ask.
  3. Step 1, costing: upload the costing model. On the Quoting tab, Costing, ROI and price approval → 1. Costing → Upload the costing model (one workbook: costs and the initial price assumptions). A changed model? Upload a new version: the earlier one stays under Earlier versions. When capital is required ($0 CER included), Finance is told to build the ROI.
  4. Record the price approval. Check the Engineering summary and Pricing history. Tick Costing completed (Cost-to-Serve) (it needs the costing model on file), set Approved by and Approved at, then Save price approval.
  5. Record capital readiness. Pick a Status (a pending status needs a justification). Click Save capital readiness.
  6. Check the terms. On Overview → Commercial terms, fill payment terms and tooling commitment, tick Terms checked and Save terms. Final Review shows an open warning if it is not done. The funding list includes $0 CER (customer-reimbursed capital) and Shared.
  7. Submit to the pricing lead. Pricers click Submit to pricing lead. It is locked for you until the lead decides or sends it back.
  8. Step 2, ROI (Finance). When the quote needs one, 2. ROI says why; Finance uploads the ROI model and the pricing lead is told. Otherwise it says Not required.
  9. Step 3, pricing lead: approve the price (G3). Ready for my review lists what was submitted. Optionally Upload an updated price table if the price changed (otherwise the price in the models stands). Choose Price approved: advance to Final Review… (it needs the costing model and, when required, the ROI model), Return to Engineering…, Return to KAM… or Hold or stop… (Hold, Rework (re-cost), Redirect, Stop). Every send-back, hold and stop needs a note. Once you advance, the pricing section is locked until a send-back reopens it.

What changed from the ERF

  • The preliminary capital figure is now the Budgetary capital estimate. There is no CER reference box.
  • No price figures are typed into QuoteFlow. You record who approved, when, the basis and the range.
  • BPR is retired. The legacy ERF # is optional.
  • Costing, then ROI, then price approval (D-001): the costing model and, when capital is required, Finance's ROI model are on file before the price is approved. Each model keeps its versions; the latest is flagged.
  • Quotes already in Quoting with costing attested before this change show No model on file and keep working.
  • You never decide Final Review on a quote you priced.

QuoteFlow quick-start: Finance (ROI)

What QuoteFlow is for you. Quotes that need an ROI before the price is approved: capital is required, $0 CER included. Pricing uploads the costing model (costs and the initial price assumptions); you build the ROI from it on your template and upload the ROI model. You are an input, not an approver: the pricing lead approves the price (G3, Price approved) once your ROI is on file.

Your home screen. ROI requests, with what to do. Your menu: Home, Pipeline. Your role reads quotes and uploads the ROI model, nothing else.

What you'll do

  1. Get told. When Pricing uploads a costing model on a quote that needs an ROI, you get a bell notice and an email: "… is ready for its ROI". It opens the quote's Quoting tab.
  2. Take the assumptions. Costing, ROI and price approval → 1. Costing: download the current costing model (the latest version is flagged current).
  3. Upload the ROI. 2. ROI says why the ROI is needed. Click Upload the ROI model. The pricing lead is told.
  4. Costing changed? If Pricing uploads a new costing model version, you are told again, and step 2 says when your ROI is older than the costing model. Upload a new version: the earlier one stays under Earlier versions.
  5. After the price is approved the steps are locked. A send-back to Quoting reopens them.

What changed

  • Assumptions come from QuoteFlow, not from email: the costing model is on the quote.
  • The ROI is a step inside Quoting, after costing and before the price is approved (D-001). No ROI is needed when capital is not required; the step says Not required and why.

Who to ask

  • Gemma Cebolla, Sr Director Pricing: the costing model and the price.
  • Bailey Schmitt, product owner. Ben Whann, builder.

QuoteFlow quick-start: Final Review approver

What QuoteFlow is for you. A one-page review of each quote Pricing submits, and a recorded decision to release the offer (gate G4, Release proposition). Approving is internal. It does not award the quote: only the customer's answer does.

Your home screen: Approvals. It shows Pending final review (G4) and Decided recently. You get an email when a quote needs you. Your menu: Home, Pipeline, Approvals, Analytics. It works on a phone.

What you'll do

  1. Open the queue. Approvals → Final Review. Each card shows price approval, capital readiness, the G2 outcome, the KAM and the pricing approver.
  2. Review. Click Review & decide: the request, the priority, the engineering solution and capacity evidence, the price approval, capital readiness, files and decisions so far.
  3. Decide. Use the bar at the bottom: Advance (release the offer), Hold, Rework (back to the affected gate) or Stop (withdraw). Stop needs a reason and a note. Rework needs a reason, a note and where it goes back to. Hold needs a note and a review date.
  4. Check the validity. Advance sets the proposal issue date (now by default) and the valid-until date.

What changed from the ERF

  • Final Review is separate from the customer outcome. Advance releases the offer; the KAM records Awarded or Lost later.
  • An open terms check or Legal review shows as a warning with a link. It does not stop you. Terms and Legal are shown read-only on the review screen.
  • After you release the offer, the terms and the capital flag are locked.
  • You cannot decide a quote where you are the KAM, or whose price you approved.

QuoteFlow quick-start: Leadership

What QuoteFlow is for you. A read-only view of every commercial capital quote: where it is, how long it takes, what we win and lose, and how the rules are followed. You see everything, including pricing and capital figures. You do not change them.

Your home screen: Leadership overview. It shows Open quotes, Median intake to G4, First-pass intake completeness, Win rate and outcome capture and Pipeline by stage. Your menu: Home, Engineering queue, Pricing queue, Pipeline, Approvals, Analytics, Admin.

What you'll do

  1. Read the analytics. Analytics → Leadership report: Pipeline, Outcomes, Cycle time, Flow, Reasons, Quality and governance, Priority, Adoption. Click the ⓘ on any chart for its definition and time window. Click Table to see the numbers.
  2. Review overrides. Admin → Override log: every override, the original value, the reason and who decided.
  3. Check the audit trail. Admin → Audit log: every view, change, decision, export and sign-in.
  4. Export. Pipeline → Export CSV (every export is audited).

What changed from the ERF

  • Outcomes are reason-coded, so win and loss reasons are reportable.
  • The win rate is shown next to the outcome capture rate.
  • Overrides are logged with a justification and can be reverted.
  • These numbers are operational, not D&A-certified.

QuoteFlow quick-start: Admin

What QuoteFlow is for you. You keep the business rules as data: value lists, reason codes, workflow labels, settings, scoring and user scope. Each change needs a reason and goes to the decision log. Seed values stay Provisional until their owner approves them.

Your home screen: Administration. Click Open admin. Your menu also has New intake, both queues, Pipeline, Approvals and Analytics.

What you'll do

  1. Change configuration. Admin → Value lists, Reason codes, Workflow labels and rules, App settings or Scoring model. Say why in "Why are you making this change?" and click Save change. Once the owner signs off, set Approval to Mark approved by me.
  2. Set user scope. Admin → Users and scope → Scope. Roles come from the SG-CCQ-* groups in Entra; ask IT to change those.
  3. Watch the integrations. Admin → Integrations: Salesforce sync runs, the Fabric mirror check and the pricing-history import.
  4. Triage UAT reports. Admin → Feedback: set status, owner and a GitHub issue link.
  5. Read the audit. Admin → Audit log, Decision log, Usage report, ERF linkage.

What changed from the ERF

  • Rules are configuration, not code. No developer is needed to change a label, a reason code or a threshold.
  • Every change is versioned with who, what, why and when.
  • No local accounts. Access comes only from Entra groups.
