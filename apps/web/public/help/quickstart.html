QuoteFlow quick-start: KAM

What QuoteFlow is for you. One place to request a Commercial Capital Quote (CCQ) and follow it to the customer's answer. You start from your Salesforce opportunity. Engineering, Pricing and Final Review do their part in QuoteFlow, and you see where it is at every step.

Your Command Center: My quotes. It shows what is Returned to me, My drafts, My open quotes by stage, Proposals expiring soon and My outcome capture rate. A quote on hold carries an orange On hold chip: rest the mouse on it, tab to it or tap it to read why it is held, who owns the hold and the review date. Your menu: Command Center, New intake, My proposals, Pipeline.

What you'll do

  1. Start an intake. Click New intake, find your Salesforce opportunity and click Use this opportunity. Four steps: Opportunity, Customer & destination, Request & items, Drawing, files & review. A red * before a label means the field is required to submit. Click Submit to CCQ. You get a quote number like CCQ-2026-00042. If Submit is refused, the message lists every field still needed, each with a Fix link that takes you to it. If your last change could not be saved, nothing is submitted and the message says so; try again. What Salesforce already knows is filled in for you and tagged from Salesforce: the customer, the destination, and on Item 1 the item name, resin, neck, container size and volume. Change any of them if they are wrong; Salesforce is not updated, and Use the Salesforce value brings it back.
  2. Attach the drawing, or give the minimum spec. On step 4, pick the File type and click Add file (on a phone it also offers the camera), or drag files onto Drawing and other files. Without a drawing you must give the minimum spec on step 3 (height, width or diameter, depth, gram weight, neck, resin, color), or the quote cannot be submitted. Those fields carry the red * until a drawing is attached. The ship-to fills from the opportunity's product in Salesforce, else from the account, and step 2 says which; a ship-to note typed on the opportunity is shown above it so you can copy from it. If the address has no city or state, add it on step 2; no reason is needed for filling a blank. Red means fix it before you can go on; yellow is worth checking and you can still go on (a key says so while one is on screen).
  3. Quote several items together. On step 3, the request (market, payment terms, dates) is asked once. Fill in Item 1 completely first (its heading says which item you are on). Below it, in the Items box, click Duplicate this item and change only what differs, or Add another item or Add from Salesforce products, for each product or variant (another size, another PCR %), and give it a name (up to 120 characters). The new item opens above the box. Each item has its own product fields, volume, need-by date and drawing (pick it under Drawings for on step 4). A refused Submit names the item to fix. Everything quoted together stays one quote, one number.
  4. A concept with no drawing yet. If the customer sent a render or a photo and relies on Pretium to design the product, pick request class Concept / innovation. The dimensions and the rest of the minimum spec become optional; fill Assumptions for this concept instead and, on step 4, add the render or photo as Sample photo / render (or a drawing). Engineering, Pricing and Final Review see the Concept badge and your assumptions, and the offer letter states them.
  5. Say what the product is. On step 3 pick the Market, then a Sub-market of that market. Both are required; the sub-market sets the strategic-fit score, and changing the market clears it. Beverage › Spirits & Mixers covers alcoholic drinks (Midas calls it Alcoholic). Tick every Decoration type the item has. If you pick Other for Product family, Components, Decoration, Resin, PCR % or Additive, say what it is in the box that opens. It is required. Stock white and Stock black colors (the colorant Pretium uses for its stock items) are offered for HDPE and PP. If Salesforce had no product details for the opportunity, the step says so: fill them in from the customer's request.
  6. Tell Pricing what it needs. On step 3, under Commercial inputs for Pricing, say what payment terms the customer asks for, whether the customer pays for any of the tooling (Customer funded (lump sum), Customer funded (amortized), Pretium funded or To be determined, the default; amortized says it needs a finance review), whether it wants returnable (multi-trip) dunnage, and whether the price is Delivered or FOB Pretium plant (with the freight type and any special arrangement, such as a drop trailer or T-bars). Freight type Other needs the Special freight arrangements. Add a Customer bid / RFP reference only if the customer gave one; most don't. All optional: a blank shows as a warning you can ignore. A payment term other than Net 15 or Net 30 says who must approve it: Non-standard: needs approval by the pricing lead (Net 45) or by Leadership (Net 60 and above, which also needs a supporting document). Who approves what is provisional. Also optional: Will the customer commit to the tooling? (Yes asks for a Legal review; Final Review sees your answer) and Does the customer have inventory or safety stock requirements? (with the weeks and a note). Every dropdown is A to Z, with Other last.
  7. Fix a returned quote. If a sales leader or Engineering returns it, you get a notification. Click it (or Fix and resubmit on the quote, on the line under the stage tracker that says whose move it is): the intake opens with who sent it back and why pinned at the top, at the review step with the first thing to fix selected (or at the field the note names). The quote keeps its number. If Pricing sent it back, you are asked whether the scope changed: No, same scope sends it straight back to Pricing; Yes sends it through qualification and Engineering again.
  8. Make the offer letter. Once Final Review approves the quote, open it (or click Offer letter on its card in My proposals). In the Offer letter card type the Attention name and a Part # for each item, check the prices (they come from Pricing's costing workbook and cannot be edited here), tick each warning and click Generate Word document. The letter downloads in the Pretium style; you can adjust it in Word before you send it. QuoteFlow sends nothing to the customer: you send the letter yourself. A sales leader or Pricing can generate it too; every other role can read and download it. Until Legal approves the wording, every letter says DRAFT at the top. If the card says the letter cannot be made yet, it says why and where to fix it.
  9. Record the customer's answer. Go to My proposals and click Record outcome (on the quote itself the button reads Record customer decision): Awarded, In negotiation, Lost, No response / Expired, On hold or Re-quote (use Re-quote while the offer is still valid). No response is not a loss. The customer says yes after the offer expired? On the quote click Start a linked re-quote: a new quote on the same opportunity, pre-filled from this one (items and drawings too), opens as an intake draft. Check every value, add the new Quote needed by date and submit; Engineering, Pricing and Final Review review it again. Each quote links to the other.
  10. Hand off a win. After Awarded, fill the Commit & hand off card (CER and receiving team; Open the CER tool and CER template link out once an Admin has set them, and Upload the CER proof takes a screenshot or PDF of the CER), then click Commit and hand off at the top of the quote → Advance (close Won). If the customer is not yet a Pretium customer, the card reminds you to request master-data setup (a help-desk ticket) during commercial activation.
  11. Find your quotes in the pipeline. On Pipeline, Filters includes Plant (quotes with an item at that plant). Columns shows what is on, in order: Move up and Move down change the order, and Views saves your filters and columns under a name (they stay in this browser). On a quote's Timeline, a file's name opens the file.

What changed from the ERF

  • Every quote starts from a Salesforce opportunity. Put everything quoted together in one quote, as items. If the opportunity already has an active quote, you are warned and can still start a separate one.
  • You no longer pick the plant, the engineer or the priority. Engineering decides the plant and engineer. The priority score is calculated and advisory.
  • The customer is the Salesforce Account on the opportunity. You don't create customer accounts: tick Not yet a Pretium customer (no ERP account) if that applies.
  • You give Quote needed by and Product needed by dates. The Salesforce close date is shown but not typed.
  • You can't change the priority tier. Ask your sales leader to override it.
  • The customer's offer letter comes from the approved quote: no retyping, and no price typed by hand.
  • Awarded is the customer's yes. The quote closes Won only after Commit & Hand Off.
  • You can see the price approval, pricing basis and capital figures on your quotes. Only Pricing changes them.
  • Once a gate is decided, its section is locked until a send-back reopens it.
  • After you record an outcome, QuoteFlow reminds you to update the Salesforce opportunity. It does not write to Salesforce. The opportunity on a quote is a link (Salesforce opportunity ↗), which opens the Salesforce app on a phone that has it; until an Admin sets the Salesforce address you see the opportunity ID.

QuoteFlow quick-start: Sales leader

What QuoteFlow is for you. You decide which new opportunities are worth engineering time (gate G1, Qualify & prioritize). You approve priority changes and see your team's pipeline and outcomes.

Your Command Center: Sales pipeline. It shows To qualify, Expiring soon (proposals you qualified whose validity ends within 7 days or has passed, soonest first), Pending override requests (only requests raised before the change), Team pipeline by stage and Proposals with no outcome past validity. A quote on hold carries an orange On hold chip. At the top are two views: My work and Analytics. Your menu: Command Center, New intake, My proposals, To qualify, Pipeline, Approvals.

What you'll do

  1. Qualify new intakes. Click To qualify (a “waiting to qualify” notice opens it on that quote’s card). The list is highest priority first. Show details on a card (or Expand all) shows what the KAM entered. Click Decide and choose Advance, Send back to… (the KAM fixes the intake; give a reason and a note), Hold (a pause, not a fix) or Stop. For a stock item or a price inquiry use Stop: not CCQ work (send elsewhere). Click Record decision. The ⓘ next to a choice explains it. A quote that is already on hold is not offered Hold again: any other decision ends the hold. A quote you sent back has nothing to decide until the KAM resubmits it; the line under its stage tracker says so.
  2. Override a priority tier. You are the only role that can. On the quote click Override next to the priority, pick the tier and give a reason of 15 or more characters. It applies at once and Engineering sees your reason on its queue. To undo it, open the quote's Timeline and click Revert.
  3. Watch your team's pipeline. Use Pipeline (List, Prioritization, Board) and the Analytics view of your Command Center. Each chart has an ⓘ with its definition.
  4. Chase missing outcomes. Proposals with no outcome past validity on your Command Center. You can record an outcome for your team. You are also told (in-app and by email) when a quote you qualified is about to expire and when it has expired. If the customer says yes after it expired, open the quote and click Start a linked re-quote (the new quote stays the KAM's).

What changed from the ERF

  • Qualification is a recorded decision with a reason, not an email.
  • The priority score orders the list. You decide.
  • You cannot qualify a deal where you are the KAM. Another sales leader decides it.
  • KAMs, Engineering and Pricing can't change the priority tier. They ask you.
  • You can read pricing and capital figures on any quote you can open. You cannot change them.

QuoteFlow quick-start: Engineering lead

What QuoteFlow is for you. One queue for all upfront engineering work on new business quotes. You assign it and decide whether there is a feasible solution (gate G2, Feasible solution?). The stage is called Engineering & Capacity: an engineer works the solution and a production scheduler works the capacity.

Your Command Center: Engineering queue. It shows Queue health, Open load per engineer, Time to assign and to decide and Returned to the KAM: top reasons. You are alerted (bell and email) when a quote arrives. At the top are two views: My work and Analytics. Your menu: Command Center, Engineering queue, Pipeline, Approvals.

What you'll do

  1. Assign work. Engineering queue → row menu … → Assign engineer (or tick rows and Assign selected…). In the same dialog, Production scheduler (optional) adds the person who works the capacity part of that quote; both are notified. Use Reassign work… to move someone's open work.
  2. Return incomplete intakes. Row menu → Return to KAM…. Say what is missing.
  3. Capacity. The production scheduler on the quote approves capacity, or blocks it with a note. A block sends the work straight back to the engineer with that note; you and the KAM are told. On a quote with no scheduler, the Approve capacity and Block capacity buttons on the Capacity tab are yours. G2 Advance needs capacity approved. Each part has its own tab: Engineering opens with Engineering recommendation (the engineer's solution, and when it was submitted), Capacity with Capacity recommendation (the sign-off, by whom and when, the note, and each capacity row in one line). A quote opens on the part waiting for you: Capacity when only capacity has been submitted, otherwise Engineering.
  4. Decide G2. Engineering tab → Decide feasibility: Advance, Advance with risk, Send back to… (the KAM, or Engineering to try another platform or site; give a reason and a note), Hold or Stop. Until the work is complete the button reads Send back, hold or stop. Capital is no longer a choice: the quote needs the Budgetary capital estimate (0 when no capital is needed), and the equipment BOM when the estimate is above $0. Hold is a pause, not a fix; its owner starts on the assigned engineer, who is told. If nobody has signed capacity off yet, tick Capacity approved (I'm signing it off) in the dialog: your approval is recorded with the decision, in one step. If capacity is blocked, the dialog shows why and the quote cannot advance. From the quote header, Decide feasibility takes you to this decision at the bottom of the Engineering tab; on the Capacity tab, Go to the decision on the Engineering tab does the same.
  5. Keep order. Pin to top… puts a quote first, with a reason. It is logged. You cannot change the priority tier: only a sales leader can.
  6. Review what your engineers submit. The engineer and the production scheduler each submit their own part; Ready for my review lists a quote once every part is in, and you are told then (provisional). With the engineering lead on the tab shows which part is in. Go to the decision at the top jumps to your decision. If you are the engineer on a quote yourself, there is nothing to submit: decide it. Decide G2, or send it back with a note. With a production scheduler on the quote the button reads Send back to…: choose the engineer or the scheduler; only that person is notified.

What changed from the ERF

  • The queue is sorted by tier, score and age. You still assign by hand.
  • You pick the engineer and the plant. The KAM no longer does.
  • You and your engineers enter the Tooling funding model and MOQ on the quote (the Commercial terms tab). Sales and Pricing no longer do.
  • Capacity evidence and your sign-off are part of the G2 record. Utilization after the new business is calculated, not typed.
  • Once you decide G2, the engineering section is locked until a send-back reopens it.
  • A hold, an advance with risk, every send-back and a stop need a note.
  • Returns go back to the KAM with a reason, and the quote keeps its number.

  • Bailey Schmitt, product owner: rules and definitions.
  • Tre Curry, coordination: access and sessions.
  • Ben Whann, builder: anything broken.
  • Found a problem? Click Report an issue in your user menu or at the bottom of any page.

QuoteFlow quick-start: Engineer

What QuoteFlow is for you. The engineering reviews assigned to you, with the intake, the drawing and the KAM's notes in one place. You record the solution. The capacity evidence is yours too unless a production scheduler is assigned to the quote: each card says whose part it is (Your part, Production scheduler's part). Your lead decides G2.

Your Command Center: My assignments. Your reviews are listed first, then the whole queue (read-only). You get a notification and an email when work is assigned to you. Your menu: Command Center, My assignments, Pipeline.

What you'll do

  1. Start the review. Open the quote. While it is in Engineering & Capacity it opens on the Engineering tab, which starts with Engineering recommendation: your solution as it stands, and whether you have submitted it. Click Start review. The line under the stage tracker says whose move it is.
  2. Record the solution. Pick the Plant / site first, then Process & machine type: the list shows only what that plant runs, and the process fills in by itself. Fill Machine platform (the machine you are quoting on: its model or line), Cavitation, Cycle time (s), Budgetary capital estimate (USD) and Tooling notes (say whether the mold has a hot runner). Click Save solution: Progress saved appears beside the button, as it does beside every Save. The product facts from intake are shown at the top: you don't retype them. Upload the equipment BOM (the one-page CSR BOM) in its own card; a new upload keeps the earlier versions. Enter 0 in the capital estimate when no capital is needed. When the estimate is above $0, the equipment BOM is needed before the quote can advance. If the part is made in two steps, tick Two-step process and fill the plants and preform number; then fill Jim's cost estimate per item: Tooling and start-up (mold lead time, the tooling lines for the process, freight and start-up pre-fill, an optional markup % only when one is noted; the totals and the customer tooling price show as you type), New-line equipment (only when a new line is needed), Direct labor (packers and operators per line) and Pack-out (method, what the parts are packed in, per layer, layers, pallet, pallets per trailer; pieces per pallet and per trailer are calculated). Leave a line blank if it is not used. Use the project total beside the capital estimate fills it from the cost lines; save the solution to keep it. Pricing reads all of it. Enter the Tooling funding model and MOQ on the quote's Commercial terms tab: they are Engineering's to fill.
  3. Add capacity evidence. Capacity has its own Capacity tab, beside Engineering. Add capacity row: site, line, utilization before, allocated annual volume, run days, assumed effectiveness, cycle time, analysis date, analyst. Click Save capacity evidence. Utilization after is calculated for you: green up to the target, amber above it, red above 100%. A number out of range is flagged as you type and cannot be saved. Download CSV exports the rows; Attach analysis adds your own file.
  4. Attach files. Attachments tab: drag CAD files, PDFs or photos onto the drop area.
  5. A quote with several items. The Engineering and Capacity tabs each show an item switcher (Item 1, Item 2 …) with what each still needs; picking an item on one tab picks it on the other. Each item has its own solution, two-step, pack-out and capacity rows; the equipment BOM and the capacity sign-off are once for the quote, and you start and submit once. Your lead cannot advance it until every item is done.
  6. Submit to your lead. When your part is ready, click Submit to engineering lead at the end of your part on the Engineering tab (the button appears once nothing is missing; until then it lists what to fill in). With a production scheduler on the quote, capacity is their part: the lead is told once you have both submitted, and each of you gets a bell notice when the other submits. Save first: while a card has unsaved changes the button is off and says "Save your changes first." It is locked for you until they decide or send it back. If capacity is blocked, the work comes back to you at once with the blocker's note at the top of the tab: rework the solution and submit again.

What changed from the ERF

  • Work comes to you by assignment, with an alert, not by email.
  • Capacity evidence is recorded with the quote, not in a separate file.
  • You can read back every figure you enter, and the pricing on the quote. Only Pricing changes pricing.

QuoteFlow quick-start: Production scheduler

What QuoteFlow is for you. The capacity part of a new business quote. The engineering lead assigns you to a quote next to the engineer. The engineer picks the plant and the machine; you say whether the line has room, and approve or block capacity. This role is new and provisional (FB-16): tell Bailey what does not fit how you work.

Your Command Center: Capacity requests. The quotes whose capacity you own, each with where capacity stands, then the whole engineering queue (read-only). Your menu: Command Center, Capacity requests, Pipeline. You can read every quote, including its figures. You change only the capacity part of the quotes assigned to you.

What you'll do

  1. Get told. New quotes show a New chip in Capacity requests until you save a capacity row (New to me lists them). You get a bell notice and an email when the lead assigns you ("capacity is assigned to you"), and again when the engineer has saved the plant and the machine type ("Capacity can start", with the plant and the machine type). You can start before that: nothing locks you out.
  2. Open your part. Capacity requests → the quote. It opens on the Capacity tab, your own tab (a dot on the tab means work is waiting for you). It opens with Capacity recommendation: whether capacity is signed off or blocked, by whom and when, the note, and each capacity row in one line. Below it: who works capacity, the engineer's solution the rows are calculated with, Capacity evidence and Capacity sign-off (marked Your part), and your Submit. The Solution is on the Engineering tab, the Engineer's part: you can read it, not change it.
  3. Add capacity evidence. Add capacity row: site (the plants that run the chosen machine type), line, utilization before, allocated annual volume, run days, assumed effectiveness, cycle time, analysis date. Click Save capacity evidence: Progress saved appears beside the button. Utilization after is calculated for you: green up to the target, amber above it, red above 100%. A number out of range (for example 750% effectiveness) is flagged as you type and cannot be saved.
  4. Approve capacity. In Capacity sign-off click Approve capacity. Capacity recommendation at the top of the tab now says Capacity is signed off. The engineering lead can then advance the quote.
  5. Or block it. Type why in Sign-off note and click Block capacity. The work goes straight back to the engineer with your note; the engineering lead and the KAM are told. When the engineer has reworked the solution, come back and approve.
  6. Submit capacity to the lead. When your rows are done, click Submit capacity to engineering lead at the end of the capacity part (it lists what is missing until then). The lead is told once the engineer has submitted too; the engineer gets a bell notice. Your rows are read-only until the lead sends them back.
  7. If the lead sends it back to you. You get a notice with the lead's note. Fix your rows, save and submit capacity again. The engineer's part stays with the lead.

What changed

  • Capacity has its own Capacity tab, apart from Engineering (Nov 19 feedback): the capacity recommendation and the engineering recommendation are shown separately, on the quote, the Overview, the Quoting tab and Final Review. The decision on the quote (G2) is still one decision, made by the engineering lead on the Engineering tab.
  • Capacity is recorded on the quote, with the solution it belongs to, not in a separate file or an email.
  • A block no longer leaves a quote waiting: it returns to the engineer at once, with the reason.
  • Changing the solution does not clear your approval. If the plant, the machine or the cycle time changes after you approved, check capacity again.

  • Your engineering lead: which quotes are yours.
  • Bailey Schmitt, product owner: rules and definitions.
  • Tre Curry, coordination: access. Ben Whann, builder: anything broken.
  • Found a problem? Tell Ben or Bailey (the Report an issue button and file uploads are not part of this role yet).

QuoteFlow quick-start: Pricing and the pricing lead

What QuoteFlow is for you. The quotes Engineering has cleared, ready to price. You upload the costing model, record the price approval and capital readiness, and the pricing lead approves the price (gate G3, Price approved) to send the quote to Final Review. Costing stays in Excel and the pricing app; QuoteFlow keeps the files. Capital readiness is its own field, not part of the gate.

Your Command Center. Analysts see Pricing desk. The pricing lead sees Pricing triage. Both show Quoting queue, Capital readiness pending and Median time in Quoting. At the top are two views: My work and Analytics. Your menu: Command Center, Pricing queue, Pipeline.

What you'll do

  1. Pricing lead: assign pricers. Pricing queue → row menu … → Assign pricer. Use Reassign work… to balance the load. You may assign a quote to yourself, price it and approve it.
  2. Read what Engineering and the KAM handed over. A quote in Quoting opens on the Quoting tab for you, and the line under the stage tracker says whose move it is. The tab starts with Engineering's hand-over: Engineering summary (decision, process, machine, plant, cavitation, cycle time, budgetary capital, capacity evidence; a row shows only when it has a value), then From engineering (equipment BOM, and per item the two-step, mold lead time, cost lines with their totals and the customer tooling price, labor and the pack-out with pieces per pallet and per trailer). Then From intake shows the payment terms the customer asked for (a non-standard request is flagged Non-standard: Pricing will review: it is for awareness and negotiation and does not change the costing), whether the customer funds any tooling, returnable or single-trip dunnage, delivered or FOB with the freight type and any special arrangement (drop trailers and T-bars are costly), the bid / program reference, whether the customer commits to the tooling and whether it wants stock held (and for how long: an extra cost). A Concept badge means the customer sent a render, not a drawing: read the KAM's Concept assumptions. A blank means the KAM did not say: ask.
  3. Step 1, costing: upload the costing model. On the Quoting tab, Costing, payback and price approval → 1. Costing → Upload the costing model (one workbook: costs and the initial price assumptions; an Excel workbook (.xlsx), up to 100 MB). A changed model? Upload a new version: the earlier one stays under Earlier versions. Uploading it does not tell Finance.
  4. Record the price approval. Check the Engineering summary and Pricing history. Costing completed (Cost-to-Serve) is ticked for you when the costing model is uploaded (untick it if the costing is not final). Set Approved by and Approved at, then Save price approval. Progress saved appears beside the button, as it does beside every Save.
  5. Check capital readiness. The Status is filled in when the quote reaches Quoting: Capital pending (budgetary) when Engineering's budgetary capital is above $0, Capital not required at $0. Change it if you need to and click Save capital readiness. Pending is normal before award (no CER is raised until the customer awards) and never holds up the price; the Note on pending capital is optional. Capital not approved means the quote should stop.
  6. Check the terms. On the Commercial terms tab, check the payment terms (the customer's request, else Net 30), pick the tooling payment, answer the tooling commitment, tick Terms checked and Save terms. A payment term other than Net 15 or Net 30 is an exception, approved or rejected with a rationale: Net 45 by the pricing lead; Net 60 and above by Leadership, with a supporting document attached on the tab (provisional). You can pre-fill the Capital terms: the approver confirms them. Funding and MOQ are Engineering's. Final Review shows an open warning if the terms check is not done.
  7. Submit to the pricing lead. Pricers click Submit to pricing lead at the top of the Quoting tab ("Your part is done?") or at the bottom: it is the same action. Until nothing is missing, the top line says what is and links to the list at the bottom. Save first: while a card has unsaved changes the button is off and says "Save your changes first." It is locked for you until the lead decides or sends it back. The lead is told in QuoteFlow and by email.
  8. Step 2, Payback: only when you ask. 2. Payback reads Not requested until someone asks. If you need Finance's payback model, the assigned pricer or the pricing lead clicks Ask Finance for a payback model (once the costing model is uploaded), with an optional note. Finance is told, and the price approval waits for Finance's upload. When it is in, whoever asked and the pricing leads are told. No longer needed? Withdraw request with a short reason: Finance is told and the price stops waiting. A hint ("Finance’s payback model may be useful here: …") is only a suggestion.
  9. Step 3, pricing lead: approve the price (G3). Ready for my review lists what was submitted. Optionally Upload an updated price table if the price changed (otherwise the price in the models stands). Click Decide price approval (it appears once the costing model and, if someone asked Finance for one, the payback model are on file; until then the button reads Send back, hold or stop) and choose Advance – price approved, Send back to… (Pricing (re-cost), Engineering, the KAM, or Pricing (another capital model)), Hold or Stop. Every send-back needs a reason and a note; a stop needs a reason and a note; a hold needs a reason, a review date and a note. Capital pending does not stop you; Capital not approved does: stop the quote, or change capital readiness. Once you advance, the pricing section is locked until a send-back reopens it.

What changed from the ERF

  • The preliminary capital figure is now the Budgetary capital estimate. There is no CER reference box.
  • No price figures are typed into QuoteFlow. You record who approved, when, the basis and the range.
  • BPR is retired. The legacy ERF # is optional.
  • Costing, then payback, then price approval (D-001): the costing model and, when someone asked Finance for one, Finance's payback model are on file before the price is approved. Each model keeps its versions; the latest is flagged.
  • Quotes already in Quoting with costing attested before this change show No model on file and keep working.
  • You never decide Final Review on a quote you priced.

QuoteFlow quick-start: Finance (payback model)

What QuoteFlow is for you. Quotes where the pricer or the pricing lead has asked for a payback model. Pricing uploads the costing model (costs and the initial price assumptions); you build the payback from it on your template and upload the payback model. You are an input, not an approver: the pricing lead approves the price (G3, Price approved), and while a request is open the price waits for your payback model.

Your Command Center. Payback requests, with what to do. Your menu: Command Center, Pipeline. Your role reads quotes, uploads the payback model and may comment on a quote; it changes nothing else.

What you'll do

  1. Get told. When the pricer or the pricing lead asks for a payback model, you get a bell notice and an email: "… needs a payback model", with their note if they wrote one. It opens the quote's Quoting tab. A costing model upload on its own does not tell you.
  2. Take the assumptions. Costing, payback and price approval → 1. Costing: download the current costing model (the latest version is flagged current).
  3. Upload the payback model. 2. Payback says who asked and when. Click Upload the payback model (an Excel workbook (.xlsx), up to 100 MB). Whoever asked and the pricing leads are told.
  4. Check the summary. After the upload, QuoteFlow shows a short summary of the headline figures it read from your workbook. It is read-only: you cannot edit it in QuoteFlow. An Excel error, a negative or zero payback, or a blank headline figure shows as a warning. A warning never blocks anything.
  5. Request withdrawn? You are told "… no longer needs a payback model", with the reason: you can stop work on it. The price no longer waits for it.
  6. Costing changed? Step 2 says when your payback model is older than the current costing model. If Pricing needs a new one, they ask again. Upload a new version: the earlier one stays under Earlier versions.
  7. After the price is approved the steps are locked. A send-back to Quoting reopens them.

What changed

  • Assumptions come from QuoteFlow, not from email: the costing model is on the quote.
  • The payback is a step inside Quoting, after costing and before the price is approved (D-001). It is needed only when the pricer or the pricing lead asks for it; until then the step says Not requested. You may still upload a payback model nobody asked for: then only the pricing leads are told.

QuoteFlow quick-start: Final Review approver

What QuoteFlow is for you. A one-page review of each quote Pricing submits, and a recorded decision to release the offer (gate G4, Release proposition). Approving is internal. It does not award the quote: only the customer's answer does.

Your Command Center: Approvals. It shows Waiting for Final Review and Decided recently; a quote on hold is listed first, with an orange On hold chip. At the top are two views: My work and Analytics. You get an email when a quote needs you. Your menu: Command Center, Pipeline, Approvals. It works on a phone.

What you'll do

  1. Open the queue. Approvals → Final Review. Each card shows price approval, capital readiness, the engineering decision, the KAM and the pricing approver.
  2. Review. Click Review & decide: the request, the priority, the engineering solution and capacity evidence, the price approval, capital readiness, files and decisions so far.
  3. Decide. Tap Decide final review (in the bar at the bottom, or again under Terms and Legal) and choose Advance (release the offer), Send back to…, Hold or Stop (withdraw). Beside the bar it says what still blocks the release; a red asterisk marks what Final Review needs (the customer tooling commitment and the capital terms). Stop needs a reason and a note. A send-back needs a reason, a note and where it goes (Send to). Hold needs a reason, a note and a review date. A quote that is already on hold is not offered Hold again: any other decision ends the hold.
  4. Check the validity. Advance sets the proposal issue date (now by default) and the valid-until date.

What changed from the ERF

  • Final Review is separate from the customer outcome. Advance releases the offer; the KAM records Awarded or Lost later.
  • An open terms check or Legal review shows as a warning with a link. It does not stop you. You edit and confirm the Capital terms on the review screen (Confirm capital terms). You cannot release the offer until you do, or while a non-standard payment-terms exception is waiting for the pricing lead or was rejected. When something blocks the release, the bottom of Open warnings says "The offer cannot be released yet. This blocks it:" and lists each item with a link to where it is fixed.
  • Capital readiness pending is normal before award (no CER is raised until the customer awards the business): it shows as a warning, never a block. A quote whose capital is not approved does not reach you.
  • After you release the offer, the terms and the capital flag are locked.
  • You cannot decide a quote where you are the KAM, or whose price you approved.

QuoteFlow quick-start: Leadership

What QuoteFlow is for you. A view of every Commercial Capital Quote (CCQ): where it is, how long it takes, what we win and lose, and how the rules are followed. You see everything, including pricing and capital figures. You do not change them. You decide two things: payment-terms exceptions of Net 60 and above, and the finance review of amortized tooling (both provisional, standing in for the CFO and Finance until those approvers are set).

Your Command Center: Leadership overview. It shows Open quotes, Median: intake to Final Review decision, First-pass intake completeness, Win rate and outcome capture and Pipeline by stage. At the top are two views: My work and Analytics. Your menu: Command Center, Engineering queue, Pricing queue, Pipeline, Approvals, Admin.

What you'll do

  1. Read the analytics. Command Center → Analytics → Leadership report: Pipeline, Outcomes, Cycle time, Flow, Reasons, Quality and governance, Priority, Adoption. Click the ⓘ on any chart for its definition and time window. Click Table to see the numbers.
  2. Review overrides. Admin → Override log: every override, the original value, the reason and who decided.
  3. Check the audit trail. Admin → Audit log: every view, change, decision, export and sign-in.
  4. Export. Pipeline → Export CSV (every export is audited).

Read the supporting document (Payment-terms approval), write a Rationale and Approve the exception or Reject. QuoteFlow quick-start: Admin

What QuoteFlow is for you. You keep the business rules as data: value lists, reason codes, workflow labels, settings, scoring and user scope. Each change needs a reason and goes to the decision log. Seed values stay Provisional until their owner approves them.

Your Command Center: Administration. Click Open admin. At the top are two views: My work and Analytics. Your menu also has New intake, both queues, Pipeline and Approvals.

What you'll do

  1. Change configuration. Admin → Value lists, Reason codes, Workflow labels and rules, App settings or Scoring model. Say why in "Why are you making this change?" and click Save change. Once the owner signs off, set Approval to Mark approved by me.
  2. Set user scope. Admin → Users and scope → Scope. Roles come from the SG-CCQ-* groups in Entra; ask IT to change those.
  3. Watch the integrations. Admin → Integrations: Salesforce sync runs, the Fabric mirror check and the pricing-history import. Set the Salesforce Lightning URL in App settings so every quote links to its Salesforce opportunity. Until it is set, quotes show the opportunity ID, and you (only Admins) see Set the Salesforce URL in Admin → Settings beside it.
  4. Triage UAT reports. Admin → Feedback: set status, owner and a GitHub issue link.
  5. Read the audit. Admin → Audit log, Decision log, Usage report, ERF linkage.

What changed from the ERF

  • Rules are configuration, not code. No developer is needed to change a label, a reason code or a threshold.
  • Every change is versioned with who, what, why and when.
  • No local accounts. Access comes only from Entra groups.
